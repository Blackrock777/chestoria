Made of Naples stone.

CheStoria's look comes from the city itself: the grey piperno stone of Gesù Nuovo's façade, the yellow tufo the city is built from, the blue of the bay, Santa Chiara's majolica tiles and Pompeian red. It's a native iOS app that borrows its best ideas from recent Apple Design Award winners.

ChangedCheStoria is now a full App Store app, not an App Clip. The QR signs at each site now open the App Store page. Onboarding takes under 30 seconds with no account, so the download is the only hurdle.

01 · Colour

Five stones and the sea

Each colour is a material you can touch in the Centro Storico, and each one has a single job in the interface. There are no extra colours.

Piperno
Ink and primary buttonsThe volcanic grey stone of Gesù Nuovo's spiky façade.#2B302E · dark mode #EEEDE8
Tufo
Brand and "reward" momentsNeapolitan yellow tuff, the stone the city is built from. Used for the shutter, the "Look closer" button, pins and the app icon.#E8B230 · always with Piperno text
Azzurro
Interactive and linksThe blue of the bay and of the city's sky-blue colours. Used for selected tabs, links and the map's water.#176FAD · dark #5AB0EA

Mostly calm stone. Tufo yellow is rare on purpose, so when it appears it means "something good is here". Green and red appear only on facts and legends.

Yellow means rewardThe shutter, "Look closer", new stickers. Never warnings, never errors.
Red means legend, not wrongWrong answers are never red. "Nope!" is playful grey text, so guessing feels safe.
The camera stays neutralOver the live camera, controls use system Liquid Glass so the real façade stays the hero.
Don'tUse Italian-flag green, white and red, or gradients on text. And don't use yellow text on white: it fails contrast.
02 · Typography

A bold voice for questions, the system font for facts

Display · Bricolage Grotesque, ExtraBold
Bank, prison or a prince's palace?

Used only for questions, answers and big headings. It's quirky and a bit hand-cut, which suits a cheeky storyteller. Open licence (OFL), so it can ship inside the app. For scripts it doesn't cover (Chinese, Japanese, Arabic, Cyrillic), the app falls back to SF Pro Rounded automatically.

Story text uses SF Pro with Dynamic Type, up to the largest accessibility sizes. It covers every language the Translation framework offers, so problem 1 is solved at the font level. The handwritten Kalam appears only in one-line reactions.

03 · References

What we take from Apple Design Award winners

For each app: the one idea we borrow, and the thing we deliberately don't copy.

2025 · Delight and Fun

CapWords

Snap an everyday object and it becomes a sticker for a word in another language.

Take: the cut-out sticker. Your "look closer" photo is lifted out of its background (Vision subject lifting) and peels into your sticker book with a white border and a haptic.

Skip: vocabulary drills. We teach one story, not a word list.

2023 · Interaction

Flighty

Live Activities, the Dynamic Island and a lot of information made very calm.

Take: a Live Activity while you walk: "A secret 40 m ahead · Gesù Nuovo". It's the nudge to look up, without opening the app.

Skip: dense data screens. A story screen has 2 facts, never a dashboard.

2023 · Visuals and Graphics

Any Distance

Collectible medals with real depth and shine, and beautiful shareable recaps.

Take: stickers with a subtle sheen that tilts with the phone (Core Motion), and a trip postcard worth sharing at the end of the stay.

Skip: stats and totals on the recap. We show stories, not numbers.

2023 · Delight and Fun

Duolingo

Instant feedback, sound and haptics that make every answer feel alive.

Take: the satisfying tap. Answer buttons have a physical "press" depth, and the reveal arrives with a spring and a haptic.

Skip: streaks, hearts and guilt. Tourists are here for 3 days, not 300.

2024 · Delight and Fun

Bears Gratitude

Gentle, warm and personal, with illustration that feels hand-made.

Take: a hand-drawn map of the Centro Storico, with your walk drawn in as you go. It feels like your own travel journal.

Skip: a mascot. Naples is already the character.

2024 · Interaction

Crouton

A clean step-by-step mode that's easy to use while your hands are busy.

Take: one thing per screen. Question, then reveal, then look closer, each full-screen with big type, easy to use one-handed in the street.

Skip: long lists. No screen in the core flow ever scrolls.

2024 · Inclusivity

Oko

Haptics and audio that work without looking at the screen.

Take: a hot/cold haptic for the "look closer" hunt (pulses speed up as you get closer), and every story can be read aloud in your language.

Skip: nothing. Accessibility is a launch requirement, not a later feature.

2024 · Social Impact

Gentler Streak

Encouraging and optimistic, with no guilt.

Take: the tone. Copy celebrates what you found and never mentions what you missed. A wrong answer gets "Good guess though".

Skip: health-style progress rings.

04 · App structure

Three tabs, camera first

The app opens straight on the camera. Everything else is one tab away.

Snap

Opens on launch. Snap → question → reveal → look closer. The core loop lives here.

My Naples

A hand-drawn map with your walk and your pins. Tap a place for tickets, hours and Then & Now.

Stickers

Your sticker book, grouped by theme. It also holds the trip postcard and settings (language, read aloud).

✦

Outside the app

A Live Activity near secrets, a "yesterday's question" widget, and an App Intent so Siri can answer "What's this place?"
